The short answer

  1. Check that the drive is really damaged. Android calls a drive damaged when it cannot read it, for example after a bootable drive was written or the drive was formatted as NTFS or ext4. Often the drive is fine.
  2. Make sure the phone sees the drive. Use a data OTG adapter, try another cable or port, and connect hard disks through a powered USB hub.
  3. Save your files first. If the drive holds files you need, copy or recover them before you change anything.
  4. Format the drive. A fresh format fixes a broken partition table or file system.
  5. Test the drive. If Bad Blocks Check finds errors, replace the drive.

1. Is the drive really damaged?

Android can read only some file systems. After a drive is formatted as NTFS or ext2/3/4, or after a bootable drive is written, Android often shows a notice that the drive is damaged or unsupported. This does not mean the drive is broken: Android simply cannot read it.

To use the drive for normal files again, format it as FAT32 or exFAT (step 4). To keep the files on it, first copy them to your phone with USB File Manager, which reads NTFS and ext drives.

2. The phone does not see the drive

  • Open the phone's Settings, search for OTG and switch it on if the option exists.
  • Use a data OTG adapter. Charge-only cables cannot carry data.
  • Plug in a FAT32 or exFAT drive and check that the phone notices it. If even that drive is not noticed, the cause is the cable, the port or the drive.
  • Test the drive on a PC, and check the phone's port for dust.
  • For hard disks, use a powered USB hub: a phone often can't supply enough power on its own.

3. Save your files before you fix the drive

Formatting erases the drive. If you need files from it, save them first. These tools only read the drive:

  • Files you can still see: copy them to your phone with USB File Manager.
  • Deleted or lost files: Recover deleted files in Ultimate USB or USB Tools scans the drive and saves the files you choose on your phone.
  • A lost partition: Recover lost partitions in Ultimate USB or USB Tools finds it and copies its files to your phone.

4. Format the drive

A broken partition table or file system is usually fixed by formatting the drive fresh. The apps do not repair a drive in place.

  1. Open Format USB in Ultimate USB or USB Tools.
  2. Choose FAT32 or exFAT. Phones, PCs and TVs can read both.
  3. Tap Format and confirm. The app tests the drive after formatting and reports success only when the test passes.

If the format does not succeed, run Wipe USB and then format the drive again.

5. Test the drive's hardware

Bad Blocks Check in Ultimate USB or USB Tools finds failing sectors and fake-capacity drives that report more space than they have. It erases the drive, so save your files first.

If it finds errors, replace the drive. Software cannot repair failed flash memory.

How to avoid this next time: what a safe USB app does

Not every USB app checks a drive before and after it writes. An app that skips these checks can leave a drive unreadable, corrupt its files, or report success when the data was never written. We often hear from people whose drive stopped working after they used such an app.

A safe USB app:

  • Has a long record. It has been on Google Play for years, with many downloads and reviews. A brand-new app has no record yet, so you cannot know how it treats your drive.
  • Checks the drive when you connect it and refuses a drive that is write-protected or does not keep data.
  • Asks before it erases anything.
  • Checks an image before it writes it to the drive.
  • Tests every format before it reports success.
  • Recovers a lost USB connection and never writes to a different drive.

Our USB apps do all of this. The USB safety guide explains each check.

What these steps cannot do

Results depend on the drive's condition. Software cannot fix a drive whose hardware has failed, and not every file can be recovered.
