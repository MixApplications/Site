Connecting a drive

The app says no device is connected

Our apps talk to the drive directly over USB, so they can open drives Android itself can't, such as NTFS, ext4 or bootable drives. The phone still has to detect the drive. Work through this in order:

  1. Open your phone's Settings, search for OTG, and switch it on if the option exists.
  2. Check your adapter is a real data OTG adapter, not a charge-only cable. This is by far the most common cause.
  3. When you plug the drive in, Android asks whether to let the app use the USB device. Tap OK. If you refused, unplug the drive and plug it in again. In Ultimate USB, DROFUS, Ventoy and USB Tools, also check the mode on the main screen is USB OTG, not SD Slot.
  4. Plug in a drive formatted as FAT32 or exFAT and check that Android notices it: most phones show a notification or a USB access prompt. Your phone's own file manager may not list NTFS or ext4 drives even when our apps can open them. If even a FAT32 drive isn't noticed, the problem is the cable, the port or the drive.
  5. Test the same drive on a PC to rule the drive out.
  6. Check the charging port for dust or lint.
Does my phone support OTG?

Most Android phones made in the last several years do. If searching Settings for OTG returns nothing, your phone most likely enables it automatically — the absence of a toggle isn't a sign it's unsupported. Confirm by plugging in a drive formatted as FAT32 or exFAT and checking your file manager.

Do I need root?

No — not for USB drives over OTG, which is what these apps are built for.

Root is only needed to reach the phone's internal SD card slot, because Android doesn't expose it the same way. If you need to work on a microSD card without root, put it in a USB card reader and connect that over OTG instead.

Can I use an external hard drive?

Flash drives, card readers, portable SSDs and OTG hubs are supported. Mechanical external hard drives (HDDs) are not officially supported — they draw more power than a phone's USB port reliably provides, so they may fail to spin up or cut out mid-operation.

If you try one anyway: charge the phone fully first, and use a powered USB hub if you have one.

How do I use the phone's SD card slot?

It needs a rooted phone. Choose SD Slot instead of USB OTG on the main screen, tap Grant and accept your root manager's prompt, then Mount. Without root, put the card in a USB card reader and use USB OTG.

What does “CRITICAL WARNING: BAD DRIVE!” mean?

When a drive is connected, the app writes a test pattern near its end and reads it back. If that fails, the drive is faulty, the cable is loose, or the drive is counterfeit and smaller than it claims, and a write is likely to fail. If the app offers to scan for the real size, tap Yes to use only the part that works, or try another cable or drive.

The drive disconnects part-way through an operation

A drive that drops out mid-write is almost always a physical connection problem, not a software one:

  • Don't move the phone or the cable while an operation is running — a fractionally loose adapter will disconnect on the smallest movement.
  • Try a different OTG adapter. Cheap adapters fail under sustained load even when they work for a quick file copy.
  • Keep the phone charged; a drive under sustained write load draws real power.
Android says the drive is corrupted or unsupported after I format it

That notification is expected after a drive is formatted as NTFS or ext2/3/4, and often after a bootable drive is written. It does not mean anything is wrong. Most Android devices have no built-in driver for these, so the system sees a drive it can't read and reports it as damaged or unsupported.

The write succeeded. Our apps read and write these file systems with their own drivers, so in Ultimate USB and Ventoy the drive still opens in USB File Manager.

Dismiss the notification and never tap the option to format — that offer comes from Android, not from us, and it will erase the drive you just prepared.

Ads & tokens

The ad won't load, or says “try again”

This is the single most common report, and it's nearly always the network — not the app. In order of how often it's the fix:

  1. Private DNS. Open Settings, search for Private DNS, choose Private DNS provider hostname and enter dns.google. Save, toggle your connection off and on, then restart the app. Ad-blocking DNS providers block the ad servers, so no ad can load.

Formatting & file systems

Which file system should I choose?
  • FAT32 — maximum compatibility (TVs, car stereos, consoles, BIOS boot). Single files are limited to 4 GB.
  • exFAT — no practical file-size limit, still read by phones, Windows and macOS. The best default for general use.
  • NTFS — for Windows. No practical file-size limit, but most Android phones can't read it.
  • ext2/3/4 — for Linux. Neither Android nor Windows reads it without extra software.

Ultimate USB and USB Tools offer all of these in Format USB. DROFUS offers FAT32, exFAT and NTFS for a Non-bootable drive, and Ventoy offers FAT32, exFAT and NTFS for its drive.

I formatted to NTFS (or ext4) and now my phone can't read the drive

The format succeeded. The limitation is Android itself: most Android devices have no built-in NTFS or ext2/3/4 support, so the system can't mount the drive even though a PC will read it perfectly. A handful of manufacturers add NTFS support, but it is the exception rather than the rule.

Ultimate USB and Ventoy still open the drive in USB File Manager, because they carry their own drivers for these file systems instead of relying on Android's.

If you need the drive readable by the phone's own file manager and other apps, use exFAT. It has no practical file-size limit and is read by Android, Windows and macOS alike.

Format failed, or the verification check failed
  1. Confirm the adapter is a data OTG adapter and reseat it.
  2. Test the drive on a PC — a drive that fails verification usually has failing flash.
  3. Try a different USB port on the adapter or a different adapter entirely.
  4. In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, run Bad Blocks Check to confirm whether the flash itself is at fault. It erases the drive.

A verification failure means data written to the drive read back differently. That is a hardware symptom, and no app can format around it.

The drive shows less capacity than it should

Two ordinary causes and one bad one:

  • Manufacturers count in decimal gigabytes; your phone counts in binary. A "64 GB" drive genuinely holds about 59 GiB.
  • A previous bootable write may have left hidden partitions. Recover the space with Partition Wizard or Format USB in Ultimate USB and USB Tools, by writing the drive with Non-bootable in DROFUS, or with Clear in Ventoy.
  • If the shortfall is large, the drive may be counterfeit. All four apps test the end of the drive when it is connected and show 🛑 CRITICAL WARNING: BAD DRIVE! if it fails.
How do I get back unallocated space?

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, open Partition Wizard, select the unallocated block and tap Create, or Delete the existing partitions and create one across the drive. Your changes are listed for review, then Apply Changes runs them together.

The quickest route, if you don't need what's on the drive: Format USB in Ultimate USB and USB Tools, or Non-bootable in DROFUS.

MBR or GPT?

GPT for modern UEFI machines. MBR for older BIOS/legacy boot, and for consoles like the PS2 that require it.

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, Partition Wizard converts between them without a PC (Convert Partition Table). In DROFUS and Ventoy you choose the Partition Table: before writing. Back the drive up first regardless — any change to a partition table is a change you don't want to get halfway through.

Bootable USB

Which app should I use to make a bootable drive?
  • DROFUS — one ISO, maximum control over the result. The straightforward choice for a Windows or Linux installer.
  • Ventoy (Unofficial) — install once, then drag as many ISOs onto the drive as it holds and pick at boot. Best if you carry several installers.
  • Ultimate USB — contains both, plus partitioning, diagnostics and the rest.
The drive was created but the PC won't boot from it

The write can be perfect and the PC still refuse it. Check, in this order:

  1. Boot menu. Actually select the USB drive in the boot menu (usually F12, F11, F9 or Esc at power-on) rather than expecting it to boot on its own.
  2. Secure Boot. Disable it in the BIOS/UEFI, or use a build that supports it.
  3. Partition scheme. GPT drives need UEFI mode; MBR drives need legacy/CSM. A mismatch means the drive simply won't appear.
  4. USB port. Try a rear port directly on the motherboard, not a front-panel or hub port.
  5. The ISO itself. Re-download it if it came from anywhere other than the official source — a truncated ISO writes fine and boots into nothing.
My Windows ISO has a file bigger than 4 GB

That's install.wim, and FAT32 can't hold it. In Ultimate USB and DROFUS, tick Split image for FAT32 compatibility. to split it into .swm parts so the drive can stay FAT32, which modern Secure Boot PCs usually need. Windows setup puts the parts back together; nothing is lost. Splitting takes a long time.

Without the tick the app uses NTFS, which has no size limit but isn't accepted by every UEFI firmware. On a Ventoy drive the ISO is copied as one file, so use exFAT or NTFS there.

How do I switch Ventoy between MBR and GPT?

In Ventoy, or in Ultimate USB under Ventoy - Unofficial, choose MBR or GPT under Partition Table:, then tap Install. You don't need a PC.

Install wipes the drive completely, including any ISOs already copied to it, so copy them back afterwards with Copy File To USB. Update keeps your ISOs but doesn't change the partition table.

How do I add or remove ISOs on a Ventoy drive?

In Ultimate USB and Ventoy, once Ventoy is installed, add ISOs with Copy File To USB, or save them straight to the drive with Download File Directly To USB FROM INTERNET. Copying them on a PC works too. No reburning, no reinstalling. Delete an ISO in USB File Manager and it leaves the boot menu.

What is Linux persistence, and why is the option greyed out?

Persistence keeps your files and changes on the USB drive across reboots of a live Linux system. In Ultimate USB and DROFUS, tick Advanced Options (Filesystem, Partition & Persistence), then set Persistent partition size. The app adds an ext3 or ext4 partition for it.

It's offered only for images that support it. For the others the app says why: the image doesn't support it, the distribution sets it up itself, or the image is written sector by sector. It also needs enough free space on the drive. On a Ventoy drive, use Add persistence in Config Hub (Ultimate USB, Ventoy).

What is Windows To Go?

In Ultimate USB and DROFUS, choosing Windows To Go instead of Standard Windows Installation puts a full Windows installation on the USB drive itself, rather than an installer for a PC. It can take several hours: use a fast drive, a fast phone and High-speed mode., and start with a full battery.

Can I burn a macOS DMG or a Raspberry Pi image?

Yes, in Ultimate USB and DROFUS (ISO Burner, Disk or ISO image), with no token cost. macOS DMG (Intel-based), Raspberry Pi OS and other ARM images are written as raw blocks, preserving the original layout and boot structure. There's no customisation applied to these, by design.

What do the Windows setup options do?

In Ultimate USB and DROFUS, Windows Setup Customization changes the installer you write. It can remove the TPM 2.0, Secure Boot and 4 GB RAM checks, drop the online Microsoft account requirement, create a local account (Windows asks for its password at first sign-in), skip the privacy questions, copy regional settings from your phone and turn off automatic BitLocker encryption. Nothing you enter leaves the phone. On a Ventoy drive, Config Hub has Windows 11: bypass hardware checks and Windows 11: bypass online account instead.

Can I theme or password-protect a Ventoy drive?

Yes, in Ultimate USB and Ventoy. Theme Manager imports a GRUB2 theme archive onto the drive, and Get more themes opens a theme website in your browser. Config Hub sets a boot-menu password, menu options and persistence. These settings are written only to the drive.

Failures & stalls

The operation is stuck or won't finish

Three causes cover almost every case:

  1. The app was minimised. Operations pause when the app leaves the foreground — that's deliberate, to protect the drive from a half-finished write. Keep it on screen and keep the screen awake.
  2. The adapter or drive. Reseat the adapter, don't move the phone while it runs, and try another adapter if it recurs. A loose or charge-only adapter is the usual culprit.
  3. No internet or an ad blocker (free version). The app shows No Internet Connection or Adblocker or Private DNS detected., and running operations wait until that's fixed. Pro doesn't pause for either.

Large drives and large images simply take time — a write that looks frozen on a slow drive is often still progressing. If the progress figure hasn't moved at all for a long stretch, treat it as a stall and check the two points above.

It stalls near the very end — 99%, or in the last few percent

The end of a write is where data reaches the far end of the drive. Stalling there is the classic signature of a drive that reports more capacity than it physically has, or whose flash is failing at the far end.

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, run Bad Blocks Check with Detect fake / counterfeit capacity ticked (it erases the drive). In DROFUS and Ventoy, reconnect the drive: if the end-of-drive test fails, the app shows 🛑 CRITICAL WARNING: BAD DRIVE! and may offer to scan for the size the drive really holds. If the drive is bad, no app can fix it.

Can the app damage my USB drive?

No. Nothing the app does is unrecoverable at the hardware level — the worst case is a drive that needs formatting again. If a drive stopped working after an operation, it was already failing.

Can I use my phone while an operation runs?

Not on other apps — operations pause when the app is minimised. Leave it in the foreground until it finishes. Long operations on large drives can take a while, so start them when you don't need the phone.

The app crashed or closed by itself

First check you installed from Google Play. Modified or repackaged copies from APK sites are a common source of crashes we can't reproduce or fix.

If it's the Play version, write to us with the app version, your phone model, the drive size and file system, and what you were doing when it closed. Crashes we can reproduce get fixed quickly.

Drive health

How do I tell if a drive is counterfeit?

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, run Bad Blocks Check with Detect fake / counterfeit capacity ticked. It writes across the whole drive and reads it back, so it erases everything on it. A counterfeit drive reports a large capacity but silently discards anything past its real size, which the test exposes. All four apps also test the end of the drive when it is connected and show 🛑 CRITICAL WARNING: BAD DRIVE! if it fails.

Symptoms before you test: files that copy successfully but are corrupt afterwards, and writes that stall near the end.

Can a corrupted drive be repaired?

Logical corruption, such as a broken partition table or file system, is usually fixed by formatting fresh: Format USB or Wipe USB in Ultimate USB and USB Tools, Non-bootable in DROFUS, or Install in Ventoy. The apps don't repair a drive in place, so copy off what you need first.

Physical flash failure isn't repairable by any software. If Bad Blocks Check (Ultimate USB, USB Tools) reports errors, replace the drive.

Does Wipe USB really erase everything?

Wipe USB (Ultimate USB, USB Tools) overwrites the whole drive once with zeros, which matches the "Clear" level in NIST SP 800-88 and defeats all normal file-recovery software. Choose Sampled (recommended) or Full verification to have it read the drive back, and save the erasure report and certificate when it finishes.

It is not a multi-pass or "Purge"-level erase, and we don't claim it is. For legally mandated destruction standards, follow your own policy.

How do I get an erasure certificate?

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, run Wipe USB. When it finishes, tap View erasure report or View erasure certificate and save it. It can't be produced again later. The certificate is made on your phone and goes nowhere unless you share it.

My drive is slower than advertised

In Ultimate USB and USB Tools, run Benchmark for real numbers. Bear in mind the whole chain limits speed: a USB 3.0 drive in a USB 2.0 adapter runs at USB 2.0 speed, and advertised figures are usually peak read, not sustained write.

Small-file writes are always dramatically slower than large sequential ones. That's how flash works, not a fault.

Theme check reads a theme and lists errors, warnings and store rules from the official theme rules, each with the screen that fixes it. It is advice only and never blocks an export. Open it from its tile in the editor, or from the button after a theme is saved.

  1. Switch on OTG in your phone's settings if the option exists. Some phones switch it off again after a while.
  2. Try another OTG adapter or cable. Charge-only adapters can't carry data.
  3. For hard disks, use a powered USB hub: a phone often can't supply enough power on its own.
  4. Check the drive on a computer, then unplug it and plug it in again.
Android asks to allow access to the USB device

Tap Allow. The app can only open the drive after you allow it. If you closed the prompt, tap Allow access on the drive's card, or unplug the drive and plug it in again. If opening fails, tap Try again.

Can I write to NTFS drives?

Yes, NTFS drives are fully readable and writable. A drive opens read-only only when Windows hibernated it (Fast Startup), when Windows did not close it cleanly and it could not be repaired, or when the drive is write-protected or refuses writes. Shut Windows down fully (not Restart or Sleep), or check the drive in Windows, then connect it again.

Copying a file larger than 4 GB fails

That is a limit of the FAT32 file system, not of the app: FAT32 can't store a single file over 4 GB. Use a drive formatted as exFAT or NTFS for large files.

Can USB File Manager format a drive?

No. USB File Manager browses and manages files; it has no formatting, partitioning or disk-image tools. To format, partition or repair a drive, use USB Tools or Ultimate USB.

Do I need root?

Not for USB drives. Root is only needed for the phone's internal SD card slot, and your root manager (such as Magisk or KernelSU) must allow the app.

How do I unplug the drive safely?

Wait for any running operation to finish, then tap Eject on the drive's card. When it says Safe to unplug, pull the drive out. If something could not be saved, the app tells you to check the drive on a computer. Unplugging during a copy can damage the files being written.

Which file systems and drives work?
