This Privacy Policy explains how the mobile game TumTum (the “Game”), published by MixApplications ("we," "us," or "our"), handles information. By downloading or playing the Game, you agree to the practices described here.

1. Overview

TumTum is a single-player puzzle game that runs on your device. It does not ask you to register, and it does not collect your name, email, contacts, photos, files, or precise location. Your game progress (levels completed, stars, best move counts, coins) and your settings (sound, music, vibration, language, crash-report and ad-personalisation choices) are stored on your device; progress and settings are kept in encrypted app storage. The advertising and consent SDKs also store their own data in the Game’s private storage (for example your consent choice, an IAB privacy string, and cached ads). If Android backup is switched on for your Google account, Android may include this data in your device backup, which Google holds for you; we never receive it. Saved progress is tied to your device model, so a backup restored to a different phone model may not restore your progress.

Permissions. The Game uses internet and network state (ads and crash reports), the advertising ID (ads), vibration (game feedback) and wake lock (keeps the screen on during a video ad). The ad SDKs add Android’s advertising-services permissions and, for one network, a fraud-prevention attestation permission. On Android 13 and later, ad SDKs may use Android’s Privacy Sandbox (Topics, Attribution Reporting and the advertising ID through Ad Services), which you can control in Settings → Security & privacy → Ads privacy. The Game requests no location, storage, contacts, camera, microphone or billing permission.

Two limited categories of data leave the device, both described below: (1) optional crash diagnostics, which you can turn off in the Game’s settings (see Sections 4, 5 and 11); and (2) advertising data used by third-party ad partners to show ads, handled with consent where required (see Sections 3 and 11).

2. No In-App Purchases

TumTum contains no in-app purchases and no paid content. Coins are earned only by playing and, optionally, by choosing to watch a rewarded ad. We do not process any payment, and we never collect or store credit-card or financial information. There is no account, no “Pro” entitlement, and no server-side purchase verification.

3. How Ads and Consent Work

Ads are provided through the Appodeal mediation platform and the advertising networks reachable through it (listed in Section 11). Before any ad is requested, the Game asks a Consent Management Platform — Appodeal’s CMP together with Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP) — to determine whether a consent prompt is required for you (for example in the EEA and the UK) and to record your choice. If consent is not given, ads are either not shown or shown without personalization. Ad requests to Google are capped at a general-audience (G) content rating, and we apply equivalent content filters for the other networks in the mediation settings.

TumTum shows ads. Google AdMob and the other advertising partners named in this policy may serve ads, collect information directly from your device, and use your device’s advertising ID (and, inside the web content of an ad, cookies or similar technologies) to show ads, limit how often you see them, measure their performance, prevent fraud and, where you have consented or the law allows, personalize them. To learn how Google uses this information, see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. You can choose whether Google shows you personalized ads in My Ad Center, and you can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in your Android settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads on some devices).

4. Data Retention and Deletion

We do not maintain a database of user profiles.

  • On-device data (progress, coins, settings) is removed when you uninstall the Game. A copy may remain in your Android device backup if backup is switched on; you manage that copy in your Google account settings.
  • Crashlytics diagnostic data is retained for up to 90 days and then deleted automatically.
  • Advertising data is retained by each advertising partner under its own policy (see Section 11).

Deletion requests: we do not offer a data deletion service. We keep no user database and no account, so we hold no data from the Game that we can find and delete for one person. Crashlytics data is deleted automatically after 90 days. To limit advertising data, reset or delete your Advertising ID in your Android settings (Settings > Google > Ads), change your ad consent in the Game (Settings → Privacy), or contact the advertising partner directly (Section 11). Uninstalling the Game removes all data it stored on your device, including your coin balance.

5. Your Choices and Controls

  • Ad personalization / “Do not sell or share.” In the Game, open Settings → Privacy. There is a control to turn off ad personalization (shown as “Personalised ads • On/Off”, or as “Do not sell or share my personal information” when your phone’s language/region setting is for the United States, for example English (United States)). Turning it off keeps ads showing but stops them being chosen using your Advertising ID. The choice is reversible.
  • Ad and data choices (consent form). Where a privacy prompt is required for your region, Settings → Privacy → Ad and data choices reopens the consent form so you can change your decision.
  • Crash reports. Crash reports are on by default. Turn them off, or on again, in Settings → Privacy → Crash reports.
  • Device controls. You can reset or delete your Advertising ID in your Android settings at any time.

10. Children and Audience

TumTum’s declared target audience on Google Play is users aged 13 and older. The Game is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.

Because an app store cannot prevent a younger user from installing an app, the Game uses Google Play’s Age Signals API to protect younger players. Google Play currently provides this signal only in some regions (for example Brazil and certain US states); elsewhere it shares nothing. Before any ad is requested, the Game asks Google Play whether an age range has been shared for the account. Google Play — not the Game — decides whether to share it, and may show its own prompt asking permission to do so.

  • If Google Play reports a user under 13, ad requests are tagged as child-directed and under the age of consent, and ads are never personalized.
  • If Google Play reports a user aged 13–17, ad requests are tagged as under the age of consent and ads are never personalized. They are not tagged child-directed, because a teenager is not a child under COPPA.
  • If Google Play reports a supervised account whose parent has not yet approved the Game, or declined it, the Game treats the user as aged 13–17.
  • If Google Play reports an adult, asks for age verification, or shares nothing at all — the account declined, the device has no Play services, or the request fails — the Game proceeds with its normal settings, where personalization still depends on your consent and on the “do not sell or share” control in Section 5.

We receive only a broad age range from Google Play. We do not save it on your device; it is used in memory for the current session to decide how ads are requested, and is queried again next time you play. If crash reports are switched on, the resulting band (child, teen or adult) may appear in the technical event log of a crash report sent to Firebase Crashlytics. We do not receive a birth date or any identity document.

TumTum is not designed for children and is not part of Google Play’s Families programme. If you believe a child under 13 has provided us information, contact us (Section 14) and we will address it.

11. Data Types Collected and Shared

To operate and monetize the Game, we collect and share specific categories of data through third-party SDKs. To align with our Google Play Data Safety declaration, we declare that we collect and/or share the following data types:

  • Diagnostics — crash logs (stack traces, device model and OS version, plus context we attach: the current level, chapter and screen, the app store the Game was installed from, and short technical event logs such as ad-consent status and the broad age band described in Section 10) and other app performance data, including errors the Game recovers from without crashing and basic session information Firebase Crashlytics collects to measure crash-free sessions. No file contents, name or contact details. Crashlytics uses a Firebase installation identifier to group reports from the same installation.
  • App interactions — used by advertising SDKs, including whether an app advertised in the Game was later installed (install attribution).
  • Approximate location — inferred by advertising SDKs from network/IP information; the Game does not request or use Android location permissions or APIs directly.
  • Device or other IDs — including the Advertising ID, the Firebase installation ID used by Crashlytics, and your IP address, which every network request carries.

The Game does not collect purchase history (there are no purchases) and does not use Firebase Analytics.

12. Security

Game progress is kept in encrypted storage inside the Game’s private app folder, which other apps cannot read. Crash reports are sent to Firebase over encrypted (HTTPS) connections. Advertising SDKs send ad requests over encrypted connections; as the mediation provider’s setup requires, some ad content (such as images or video files) may be loaded over ordinary HTTP. We run no server of our own for TumTum and keep no copy of your data, so there is no account or database of ours that could be breached.
