Privacy Policy — Cofferlock

This policy applies to the Cofferlock app (com.mixapplications.cofferlock) published by MixApplications. It is specific to Cofferlock; other MixApplications apps are covered by the general privacy policy. Where the two differ, this page applies to Cofferlock.

Last updated: October 4, 2026
The short version

Cofferlock is an encrypted file vault, and your files never leave your device unless you send them yourself. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no crash-reporting service. To tell you when a newer version is available, it asks Google Firebase, which receives a random installation identifier and basic app details. If you buy Pro or the Supporter edition, a purchase token is sent so that Google Play can confirm the purchase; it is not kept. Your files are encrypted on your device inside a vault file that you place yourself, or that you keep in the App’s own private storage. We cannot see, open, recover or decrypt your vault. Purchases are processed by Google Play.

This Privacy Policy explains how the mobile app Cofferlock (the “App”), published by MixApplications ("we," "us," or "our"), handles information. By downloading or using the App, you agree to the practices described here.

1. Overview

Cofferlock stores files you choose inside an encrypted vault on your device. It contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software, and it has no user accounts.

The App uses the internet for the following.

Update check. When the App starts, it asks Google Firebase Remote Config whether a newer version of Cofferlock is available or required. As Google documents for Firebase (Firebase data disclosure), Firebase collects with it a random Firebase installation identifier (created for this installation of the App; Google states it does not uniquely identify a user or a physical device), your device’s model, brand and Android version, its country, language and time zone, and the App’s package name and version. The answer is only the latest version numbers and the Google Play link. See also Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Purchase check. If you buy Pro or the Supporter edition, the App sends that purchase’s token, the product and the App’s name to our purchase check, which asks Google Play whether the purchase is valid. See Section 4.

Any other connection the App makes carries nothing about you, your device or your files. We do not receive your files, file or folder names, passwords, recovery phrase, biometric data, usage data, location or contacts.

2. Where Your Data Is

  • Your files and their names are stored only inside the encrypted vault file that you create and place on your device’s storage (or SD card) with the Android file picker. If you choose Keep it inside the app instead, the vault file is kept in the App’s private storage on your device, where file managers and other apps cannot see it; it is still encrypted, never uploaded and excluded from Android backups. Every block of the vault is encrypted with AES-256-GCM.
  • Your password and 12-word recovery phrase are used on your device to derive the encryption keys (Argon2id). They are never stored in readable form and never leave your device.
  • App settings, the list of vault files you opened, and a local log of failed unlock attempts (time and count only; no photo is ever taken) are kept in the App’s private storage on your device.
  • We have no copy of your keys. We cannot open, recover or decrypt a vault, and there is no backdoor. If you lose both your password and your recovery phrase, the vault cannot be opened by anyone, including us.

3. Permissions

Cofferlock uses the biometric permission so that you can unlock with your fingerprint (on Android 9 and later, with a strong fingerprint sensor). Face unlock is not supported. Biometric data is handled by Android and is never available to the App; a successful scan only releases a key kept in your device’s secure hardware (Android Keystore).

The App declares the internet permission for the connections described in Section 1. The Google libraries it uses add their own standard entries, as their documentation describes: Google Play Billing adds the in-app purchase permission (to ask the Play Store app about your purchases) and network-state access; Google Firebase adds a wake-lock permission and the Firebase Cloud Messaging receive permission. Cofferlock receives no push messages and shows no notifications. On older Android versions the fingerprint permission is also listed for the biometric unlock. The App requests no storage, camera, microphone, location, contacts or notification permission. Files are added only through the Android file picker, one choice at a time.

4. Purchases

The one-time Pro and Supporter purchases are processed by Google Play under the Google privacy policy. The App asks the Google Play app on your device which purchases you own. When a purchase is new, the App sends its purchase token, the product (Pro or Supporter) and the App’s package name over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to our purchase check, a small program we run on Google Cloud (Firebase Cloud Functions, servers in the United States). It asks Google Play whether that purchase is valid and answers the App with a signed result. Nothing else is sent by the App: no name, email, file or vault data. As Google documents for Firebase Cloud Functions, the call also carries your connection’s IP address and a Firebase messaging token (an app-installation identifier); Cofferlock sends no messages with it. We do not store the token or the answer; Google Cloud keeps its usual technical request logs.

We never receive your payment card or banking details. Google provides us with transaction records (such as order number, product, price, date and country) in the Google Play Console, which we use only for accounting, tax and refunds.

5. Diagnostics You Choose to Share

If the App crashes, it writes a short crash log in its private storage on your device. The log contains the App version, the device manufacturer and model, the Android version and technical error details. File and folder names from your vault and document addresses are kept out of it.

The log leaves your device only if you open Settings → About → Share diagnostics and send it with an app you choose (for example your email app). Nothing is ever sent automatically. We use a log you send only to fix the problem you reported.

6. Files You Open or Share With Other Apps

When you tap Open, Share, Copy out or Send as sealed envelope, Cofferlock hands the file you selected to the app you pick, through Android’s standard share and “open with” system. Files opened this way are decrypted while the other app reads them and are not written to storage in readable form by Cofferlock; access ends when the vault locks. What the receiving app does with a file is governed by that app’s own privacy policy. A copy you save outside the vault is an ordinary, unencrypted file.

7. Support

The Support button opens our contact page in your browser. If you contact us there or through WhatsApp, we receive what you send (such as your email address or phone number and your message) and use it only to answer you.

8. Backups

Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer of the App’s own data are disabled. Your vault is a single file: copies of it that you make yourself (to another folder, an SD card or a cloud drive of your choice) stay fully encrypted and can be opened only with your password or recovery phrase. A vault kept inside the App is part of the App’s private data, so it is excluded from Android backups too; make your own copy with Settings → Copy file (backup).

9. Data Retention and Deletion

We do not maintain a database of users.

  • On-device data (your vault files, settings, attempt log and crash log) stays on your device. Delete your vault file(s) with your file manager and uninstall the App to remove all of it. Uninstalling alone does not delete vault files you placed in shared storage. A vault kept inside the App is deleted permanently when you uninstall the App or clear its data, and nobody, including us, can recover it.
  • Support conversations and diagnostics logs you send us are kept only as long as needed to resolve your request and are deleted when the support case is closed.
  • Purchase checks are answered and not stored by us (Section 4).
  • The Firebase installation identifier is kept by Google Firebase as Google describes in Privacy and Security in Firebase; uninstalling the App ends its use. We do not store it.
  • Purchase records are held by Google Play under Google’s policy; the transaction records we see in the Play Console are kept as long as tax and accounting law requires.

Right to request deletion: to have a support conversation or a diagnostics log you sent us deleted, contact us using the details in Section 18.

16. Security

Vault contents are encrypted with AES-256-GCM per 4 KiB block, with keys derived from your password by Argon2id and held in independent key slots for the password, the recovery phrase and biometric unlock. Screenshots are blocked by default, the App locks automatically, and wrong attempts are slowed down. No method of protection is perfect: the App cannot protect a vault on a rooted or compromised phone while it is unlocked, and it cannot protect a weak password. The App’s What this app does not protect against page lists these limits.
